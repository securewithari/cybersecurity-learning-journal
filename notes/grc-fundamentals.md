## 🏛️ What is GRC?

GRC stands for **Governance, Risk, and Compliance**. It is the framework 
that organizations use to:
- **Govern** — set policies and leadership direction for security
- **Manage Risk** — identify, assess, and treat threats to the organization
- **Stay Compliant** — meet legal, regulatory, and industry requirements

GRC is the backbone of how organizations protect themselves strategically,
not just technically.

## 🔐 The CIA Triad

The foundation of all cybersecurity decisions:

| Principle | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Confidentiality** | Only authorized people can access data | Encryption, access controls |
| **Integrity** | Data is accurate and hasn't been tampered with | Hashing, audit logs |
| **Availability** | Systems and data are accessible when needed | Backups, disaster recovery |

## 🗺️ NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)

The NIST CSF is a voluntary framework created by the U.S. government to 
help organizations manage cybersecurity risk. It is organized into 
**5 core functions**:

### 1. 🔍 Identify
Understand your assets, risks, and environment.
- Asset inventory
- Risk assessments
- Business impact analysis

### 2. 🛡️ Protect
Put safeguards in place to limit the impact of threats.
- Access controls (IAM)
- Security awareness training
- Data encryption

### 3. 👁️ Detect
Identify when a cybersecurity event has occurred.
- SIEM monitoring (e.g., Sumo Logic)
- Intrusion detection systems
- Log analysis

### 4. 🚨 Respond
Take action when an incident is detected.
- Incident response plans
- Communication procedures
- Containment strategies

### 5. 🔄 Recover
Restore systems and learn from the incident.
- Disaster recovery plans
- Business continuity planning
- Post-incident reviews

## 🔑 Key GRC Terms to Know

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Risk** | The potential for loss or harm from a threat |
| **Threat** | Anything that could cause harm (hacker, fire, human error) |
| **Vulnerability** | A weakness that a threat could exploit |
| **Control** | A safeguard put in place to reduce risk |
| **Residual Risk** | Risk that remains after controls are applied |
| **RTO** | Recovery Time Objective — how fast you must restore a system |
| **RPO** | Recovery Point Objective — how much data loss is acceptable |
| **BIA** | Business Impact Analysis — identifies critical processes |
